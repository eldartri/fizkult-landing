01 How it works

Two minutes —
and your coach is online

Nothing to enter by hand, nothing to set up. From there it speaks first.

01

Connect your Garmin

Two minutes in the bot: tap the link, allow access — done. Just leave the Historical Data box ticked: with it your whole past history comes across, and your coach knows your baseline from day one.

02

It works out your baseline

How much you usually sleep, what your resting heart rate usually is, how much you usually train. From there it compares you with yourself — and tells you where today has drifted from your own usual, not from somebody else's table of norms.

03

Then it writes first

Every morning — how the night went and what to do today. After a workout — a breakdown. On Sundays — the week. You can ask at any moment: it's in the same chat.

04 For coaches

Coaching people?
There's the Coach's room

You see your athletes every morning — not once a week at a session. In one letter, without the "so how are you feeling today" round.

A morning summary for the whole group

One letter every morning: each athlete's sleep and heart rate for the night, workouts you hadn't heard about yet, and for anyone with no numbers today — the date of their last data. "No data" and "did nothing" are different lines.

Invitations by link

You send a link to the people you coach. Each of them decides whether to open access, and can revoke it at any moment — at which point they simply drop out of the summary.

Plans straight to the athlete

You send a plan from the Coach's room — it arrives in the athlete's chat and they accept or decline. The card shows the state: awaiting an answer, accepted, declined.

A boundary both of you can see

You get the numbers: their state and the facts of their workouts. You do not see their Morning, their breakdowns or their conversations with the coach — those stay between them and the coach in the chat. The athlete reads this same text on the consent screen, before they agree.

05 For clubs and teams

A bot in your group
that counts consistency

A running club, a cycling team, a chat of friends who promised each other they wouldn't fade out in November.

01

One rule

A day with a workout in it counts towards the season. Not kilometres and not pace — days. The fast and the slow are equal in this chat, and that's deliberate: consistency isn't held together by speed.

02

Season and tiers

A season runs to its own date, and everyone has their own tier: Start, Rhythm, Stride. It's not a podium — we mark turning up, not placing. You can ask for the table in the chat at any moment.

03

Nobody appears without saying so

Until a person has chosen how they want to be shown, they aren't in the table at all — not by name, not as a line. The club puts nobody's data into the group chat without permission.

08 Straight up

What Fizkult is —
and what it doesn't do

Fizkult is a coach that knows your numbers every day, remembers what you allowed it to remember, and tells you in plain words what to do.

Fizkult is not a doctor or a replacement for a human coach. It makes no diagnoses and gives no medical advice. If you have your own coach, Fizkult works with their plan, not against it.

And it doesn't make things up. If no data came in for the night, it will say there's nothing to compare today against rather than slot in a nice-looking number. The marks in the letter say exactly that: 🟢 as usual · 🟡 noticeably off · 🔴 well off · ⚪ not comparing today.

09 Questions

What people ask
before starting

Do I need a Garmin?

Yes, everything rests on it: Fizkult takes workouts, sleep and heart rate straight from the watch, with nothing to enter by hand. Connecting takes two minutes, and there's exactly one place in it worth paying attention to.

The Historical Data box on the connection page. With it your whole past history comes across — and your coach knows your baseline from day one. Without it, only new workouts, and there'll be nothing to compare you against. Also sync your watch in Garmin Connect before connecting: if it hasn't handed data to the app in a while, there'll be nothing to collect.

My watch already has readiness and a suggestion for the day. Why this as well?

The number on your watch is a good thing, and we're not arguing with it. But it's calculated by one algorithm for everybody and it lives inside an app: it doesn't know you have a race in six weeks, that yesterday you wrote about a sore knee, what your coach sent for this week, or that you already moved your intervals last week.

Fizkult holds all of that in mind and speaks in decisions rather than numbers — why today looks like this, what it will give you and when you go back to the work. And you can argue with it: say "hate to wreck the week" and get an explanation instead of the same number again.

Will it write me a training plan?

No, and it's fairer to say so before you pay. The plan is written by you or your coach. Fizkult takes it as text — a list from your notes, a table from a messenger — keeps it in front of it every day, marks off what's done and tells you when your state says a day is worth shifting.

If you have no plan at all, it still works: it looks at your week and tells you what to do today. But it won't write the week-by-week build-up to your race for you.

I have a Polar, a Suunto or an Apple Watch

For now Fizkult connects only to Garmin — said plainly, without "it's all coming soon" and without dates we don't have. When other watches arrive, we'll announce it in the bot and here, on this page.

What arrives on the very first morning?

The first morning after your history has come across and you've had a first night with the watch on: sleep with a verdict on the night, recovery and readiness, the week's workouts, yesterday, and the coach's words on what to do with all of it today. Plus the question of how you feel — it takes your answer into account too.

While there are still few nights, it says so: there isn't enough to work out a baseline from. The baseline builds up over the first weeks — and from then on the comparison runs against it.

I'm an amateur, not an athlete. Is this for me?

Fizkult is built for exactly this: you train a few times a week, you put your own week together and you don't always know when to push and when to rest. A pro with a team of coaches doesn't need it. Someone with nobody supporting them between sessions — very much does.

I already have a coach. Why would I need this?

Your coach sees you once a week — Fizkult looks at your morning every day in between. It works with your coach's plan, not against it.

And if your coach is on Fizkult too, they can open the Coach's room: send you a plan straight into the chat and see a summary of all their athletes every morning. You open the access yourself and revoke it whenever you like — more on that above.

I'm a coach / I have a club

We open coach status and clubs in Telegram groups by hand, on request — that way we know who's behind it. Write to the bot and tell us who and how many you coach, or what your group is, and we'll answer in the same chat.

How do I cancel the subscription?

You don't — and that isn't a dodge. We don't set up recurring charges: a payment runs for its term, a month or a year, whichever you chose, and then simply ends. Don't renew and access closes, with nobody taking money off your card just in case. The trial week commits you to even less: we don't ask for a card at the start.

Payment and refund terms are in the offer agreement (in Russian).

Is this a doctor?

No. Fizkult makes no diagnoses and gives no medical advice. It draws coaching conclusions — "you've overloaded, time to back off" — and when a conclusion is serious it tells you itself how long to wait and what sign to take to a doctor. If something hurts or isn't recovering, see a doctor first.

What about privacy?

The numbers from your watch work only on your recommendations. We don't sell them and don't pass them to third parties.

Inside the bot everything is open: /data — exactly what came across from the watch, /memory — what the coach remembers about you and what can be erased, /team — who sees your data and how to revoke access. In detail — in the personal data policy (in Russian).
