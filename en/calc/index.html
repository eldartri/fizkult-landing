Running calculators

A calculator that hands you one number and goes quiet leaves you exactly where you started. These four do the same arithmetic as any other, and then explain what the number is built from, what data the formula stands on, and where it is known to be wrong. Nothing is sent anywhere: the maths runs in your browser and the fields stay with you.

Pace

Running pace calculator

Give it two of the three and it returns the third: distance and time give you pace and speed; distance and pace give you the finish time. Plus a split table in miles or kilometers with 5K, 10K, half marathon and marathon marks, which you can rebuild as a negative split.

Open
Race time

Race time predictor

Turns one race result into predictions for every other distance, using Riegel’s formula. Give it two results and it works out your own endurance exponent instead of the textbook 1.06. The marathon row is flagged on its own: that is where the formula runs systematically fast, and the page says so rather than hiding it.

Open
Heart rate zones

Heart Rate Zone Calculator

Works out zone boundaries two ways at once — as a share of maximum heart rate and as a share of heart rate reserve — and shows which zone your run falls into on each scale. The age-predicted maximum comes not from "220 minus age" but from Tanaka 2001: 351 studies, 18,712 people.

Open
BMI

BMI Calculator

Body mass index, and a second measure alongside it — waist-to-height ratio, the one muscle throws off far less. The WHO bands are shown not as index numbers but as a corridor of pounds for your height, so you can see at once that forty-five pounds fit inside "normal weight".
